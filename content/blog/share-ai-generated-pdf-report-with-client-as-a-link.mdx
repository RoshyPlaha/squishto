# Share an AI-Generated PDF Report With a Client as a Link and See When It's Opened

![Folded coral page, dots and squiggles, illustrating a PDF report shared as a link](/blog/share-ai-generated-pdf-report-with-client-as-a-link.svg)

To share an AI-generated PDF report as a link, save the PDF from your AI tool, upload it at [squish.to/upload](/upload), and send the short link you get back. Your client opens it in their browser with no login. The stats page then shows when the link was opened, so you know when to follow up.

This works the same whichever tool made the report. ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Microsoft Copilot, Perplexity, NotebookLM, Gamma and others can all produce a PDF, and the steps below do not change.

## Why a link beats an attachment for an AI report

Most AI tools hand you a file to download. That is fine for you, but sending it on has three small snags:

- **Attachments get lost.** A PDF in an email thread is hard to find a week later. A short link is easy to paste into chat or say out loud.
- **A chat share link is not the report.** Some tools can share the whole conversation, but your client then sees the chat, not a clean document.
- **You get no signal.** An attachment tells you nothing about whether it was opened. A link with stats does.

## Step 1: Get a clean PDF from your AI tool

Ask for a report that reads well on its own. A prompt that works across tools:

> Turn these notes into a two-page client report. Start with a three-sentence summary, then results, then next steps. Use plain headings and short paragraphs. Do not invent numbers. If a figure is missing, write "to confirm".

A few habits save embarrassment:

- **Check every figure.** AI tools can produce confident numbers that are wrong. Compare each one with your source data before the report leaves your hands.
- **Keep it short.** Two to four pages gets read. Fifteen gets skimmed.
- **Look at the PDF itself.** Open it once and check page breaks, fonts and images before uploading.

If your tool only gives you HTML or a slide deck, you can print it to PDF from the browser, or upload the HTML as a page instead. See [hosting AI-generated HTML and tracking views](/blog/host-ai-generated-html-track-views).

## Step 2: Upload and name the link

1. Open [squish.to/upload](/upload) and choose your PDF.
2. Choose a custom name, such as `report-acme-nov`. Names use letters, numbers and hyphens, from 3 to 30 characters.
3. Copy the short link. You also get a QR code, handy for a printed handout.

The limits to know: files can be up to 10MB, uploaded files stay live for 30 days, and you do not need an account. If the client might come back after 30 days, re-upload the report and send the new link.

## Step 3: One upload per recipient

Stats are per link, not per person. If three people at the client get the same link, you will see opens but not who made them. To know who opened the report, upload a separate copy for each person, for example `report-acme-priya` and `report-acme-tom`. The full approach is in [one link per recipient to track who opened a proposal](/blog/one-link-per-recipient-track-who-opened-your-proposal).

## What the stats show

Open **View stats** or visit squish.to/stats and enter your link name. You will see total opens, opens in the last 7 days, your busiest day, a 14-day chart, and recent opens with the source and country.

## What an "open" means for a PDF

Be straight with yourself about what the number says:

- **It counts visits to the link.** An open means someone followed your short link. It does not tell you how many pages they read or how long they stayed.
- **A saved copy is invisible.** If the client downloads the PDF and forwards that file, those later reads do not show up. Only visits to your link do.
- **Previews can add opens.** Some chat apps and email scanners load links automatically to build a preview. An open seconds after you hit send may be one of these. Later opens, from a different source or country, are the better sign that a person looked.

Used this way, the numbers are a good nudge: no opens after a few days is a reason to check the email arrived, and a fresh open is a friendly moment to get in touch.

## A short message to send with it

> Here is the November report for Acme. It is a two-page summary with next steps on the last page. squish.to/report-acme-priya

Say what it is and how long it takes to read, and let the link do the rest.

## FAQ

**How do I share an AI-generated PDF as a link?**
Save the PDF from your AI tool, upload it at squish.to/upload, and send the short link. Anyone with the link can open it in their browser without logging in.

**Can I see when a client opens the PDF?**
Yes. The stats page shows total opens, the last 7 days, your busiest day, a 14-day chart, and recent opens with source and country.

**Does it show how many pages they read?**
No. It counts visits to the link, not reading time or pages.

**Can I tell which person opened it?**
Not from one link. Upload one copy per recipient to tell them apart.

**How big can the file be, and how long does it last?**
Up to 10MB, and uploaded files stay live for 30 days.

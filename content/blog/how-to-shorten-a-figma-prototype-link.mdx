To shorten a Figma prototype link, share the prototype from Figma, copy the link, and paste it into a URL shortener like [squish.to](https://squish.to). You get a short link that opens the same prototype. Figma doesn't offer its own shortener, so a separate tool is how you get a link you can say out loud or put on a slide. Shortening only changes how the link looks, and who can open the prototype is still controlled by Figma's sharing settings.

The one thing worth checking before you send it is where in Figma you made the link. A prototype link and a plain file link are not the same thing.

## Step 1: Create the link from the prototype

1. Open your file in Figma and start the prototype (presentation view).
2. Click **Share prototype**.
3. Set the access and permissions, then copy the link.

Figma's community guidance says to copy the link from inside the prototype. A link copied from the design canvas can behave differently for the person opening it, for example by asking them to sign in or by opening the file rather than the prototype. Choosing the "Anyone with the link" option, rather than a company-only one, is what lets people outside your organization open it. Menu names and options move around between Figma updates and plans, so I couldn't confirm every label for this post. Check what you see in your own account.

## Step 2: Check the plan and setting that fit your job

A few things depend on your Figma plan and your organization's settings, based on Figma's help pages and community forum posts I read for this post:

- **Prototype-only links.** Figma's help says these can be shared on any plan, though forum threads suggest a prototype with different access from its file may need a paid plan.
- **Password protection.** Reported as available on Professional, Organization and Enterprise plans.
- **Organization rules.** Admins can restrict public sharing, and in that case a public short link will not change that.

If you are sending to a client who has no Figma account, open the short link in a private window first. If it opens without a sign-in, anyone you give it to can see it.

## Step 3: Paste it into squish.to and name it

1. Go to [squish.to](https://squish.to) and paste your Figma prototype link.
2. Choose your own ending, such as `squish.to/checkout-flow-v2` or `squish.to/acme-onboarding`. Custom endings run 3 to 30 characters, using letters, numbers and hyphens, and they are case-sensitive, so lowercase is safest.
3. Send it in an email, a Slack message, a deck or a handoff doc. Every squish.to link also comes with its own [QR code](/blog/qr-codes-for-every-link), which is handy for testing a mobile prototype on a real phone.

No account is needed, and each link has click stats, so you can see whether the prototype is being opened.

## Does shortening change who can open my prototype?

No. A short link is a signpost to your Figma address. It does not add or remove access. If the Figma link is restricted, the short link leads to Figma's own sign-in or access screen. Squish.to is not connected to your Figma account, so it never sees your designs or your sharing settings. More in [does shortening a link protect a sensitive URL](/blog/does-shortening-a-link-protect-it).

## Small tips for smoother design handoffs

- **Name the ending after the flow.** `squish.to/signup-flow` tells reviewers what they are about to open.
- **Version your names.** If you share a new prototype link for each round, use `-v2`, `-v3` so old feedback links don't get mixed up.
- **Keep the same link when you can.** If you update the same prototype in Figma, the Figma link normally stays the same, so the short link keeps working. A new prototype link needs a new short link.
- **Share the right link for the audience.** Reviewers want the prototype. Developers usually want the file. Send each their own.

## Quick answers

**Does Figma have a built-in link shortener?** I found no sign of one in Figma's help pages or community forum, where people have asked for shorter prototype URLs for years.

**Will my client need a Figma account?** It depends on how the link is set up and on your plan and organization settings. Test in a private window.

**Is there a cost?** squish.to is free to use, with no account required.

**Is Figma the only tool this works for?** No. The same idea applies to [Dropbox links](/blog/how-to-shorten-a-dropbox-link), [OneDrive and SharePoint links](/blog/how-to-shorten-a-onedrive-or-sharepoint-link) and more.

Ready to try it? Paste your Figma prototype link into [squish.to](https://squish.to), pick an ending your reviewers will recognise, and send it.

# Share a Google Doc Before the Meeting: Name the Doc, Name the Link

To share a Google Doc ahead of a meeting, give the doc a clear name, set the role people need (Viewer to read, Commenter to give feedback, Editor to co-write), copy the link, and paste it into [squish.to](https://squish.to) with a short ending that matches the doc, like `squish.to/q4-planning-prereads`. People can read the link before they open it, remember it, and find it again in the invite.

The rest of this post is a checklist you can run in about two minutes, plus a couple of Google link tricks that pair well with a short link.

## Step 1: Name the doc like someone else will search for it

Your attendees will not see your file list. They will see the doc title in the tab, in the email preview and in their own Drive under "Shared with me". A title like "Untitled document" or "Notes v3 FINAL" does you no favours.

A pattern that works: **what it is, for what, and when**. For example:

- `Q4 Planning: Pre-read (Oct 21)`
- `Onboarding Handbook: Comments welcome by Friday`
- `Acme Kickoff: Agenda and Goals`

Keep it short enough to fit in a browser tab. The name is the first impression of the meeting, so it is worth ten seconds.

## Step 2: Pick the role that matches what you want people to do

In the Docs **Share** dialog, choose who can open the file, then the role:

- **Viewer** for a pre-read, a handout or an agenda people only need to read.
- **Commenter** for feedback ahead of time. People can leave comments and suggestions without changing your text.
- **Editor** for real co-writing, such as a shared agenda or meeting notes.

The role matters more than people think. If you want comments before the meeting, Commenter gives you them without the risk of someone accidentally deleting a paragraph. Reserve Editor for people who are actually writing.

For who can open it, **Restricted** means only people you add by email, and **Anyone with the link** means anyone holding the URL. Pick Restricted for anything sensitive. If you pick "Anyone with the link", assume the link may travel. For more on this, see [how to shorten a Google Drive link](/blog/how-to-shorten-a-google-drive-link).

## Step 3: Choose how the link opens

The end of a Google Docs link controls what the recipient sees first. With a short link, you decide this once and everybody gets the same experience.

- **`/edit`** is what you get by default. It opens the doc ready to work in.
- **`/preview`** opens a clean, read-only view with the editing menus removed. It is a good fit for a pre-read.
- **`/copy`** prompts the recipient to make their own copy of the doc. It is useful for templates, such as a meeting notes template or a worksheet.

A few honest limits. These are conventions in the link, not security settings. Someone who is signed in with edit access may still land in edit mode, and anyone can change the ending in the address bar. If you need to truly control access, use the Share settings and keep the doc Restricted. Use the link ending for convenience, not protection.

## Step 4: Shorten it with a name that says what it is

Google's raw link is a long string of characters. It looks messy in a calendar invite and is impossible to read aloud. Paste your final link, with the ending you want, into [squish.to](https://squish.to), and choose your own ending. Custom endings use letters, numbers and hyphens, from 3 to 30 characters.

Do this last, after the doc name and the link ending are settled, so the short link always matches what it opens.

Good short endings read like the title:

- `squish.to/q4-planning-prereads`
- `squish.to/onboarding-feedback`
- `squish.to/acme-kickoff`

Lowercase with hyphens is the safest. If people will hear the link on a call, see [how to build a short link you can say out loud](/blog/short-links-you-can-say-out-loud).

## Step 5: Test it the way a stranger would

Open the short link in a private or incognito window. If the doc opens, anyone with the link can open it. If you are asked to sign in, the doc is Restricted and your attendees will need to be signed in with the Google account you shared it with. This one test catches most "Request access" emails before they reach you.

## What the short link stats can and can't tell you

Every squish.to link has a stats page, so you can see how many times the link was opened, the last 7 days, a 14-day chart, and recent opens with country and source. It is a simple way to check whether people have used the link you put in the invite.

It is not a record of who read the doc. Stats are per link, not per person, and link previews or email scanners can add opens that no human made. The stats count visits to the short link, not what happened inside Google Docs. For who has opened or edited a document, use Google's own tools.

## A quick pre-meeting routine

1. Name the doc so it makes sense in a tab.
2. Set the role: Viewer, Commenter or Editor.
3. Decide the link ending: `/preview` for reading, `/copy` for templates.
4. Shorten it with a readable ending.
5. Open it in a private window.
6. Put the short link in the invite title or description, not just the body.

## FAQ

**How do I share a Google Doc before a meeting?**
Name the doc clearly, set the role people need in the Share dialog, copy the link, and send it in the invite or a message. A short link makes it easier to read and remember.

**Which role should I choose for a pre-read?**
Viewer if people only need to read, or Commenter if you want feedback before the meeting. Use Editor only for people who will actually change the text.

**Can I make people copy the doc instead of editing it?**
Change the end of the link from `/edit` to `/copy`. People are prompted to make their own copy. It is a convenience, not a lock, so keep the Share settings in order too.

**Will a short link change who can open my doc?**
No. A short link just points to the Google URL. Google's sharing settings still decide who gets in.

**Do I need an account to use squish.to?**
No. Paste the link, choose an ending and share it.

To shorten a OneDrive or SharePoint link, create the share link in OneDrive, copy it, and paste it into a URL shortener like [squish.to](https://squish.to). You get a short link that opens the same file or folder. Shortening only changes how the link looks. Who can open the file is still decided by the link type you picked in OneDrive.

That link type is the one thing worth checking before you send anything. A link for **Anyone**, a link for **People in your organization** and a link for **Specific people** can all look equally long, but they open for very different audiences.

## Step 1: Create the link in OneDrive first

1. In OneDrive on the web, select the file or folder and choose **Share**.
2. Open the link settings and pick who the link works for. Microsoft's help pages describe options such as Anyone, People in your organization, and Specific people. Which of these you see depends on your account type and on what your organisation's admin allows, so confirm in your own account.
3. Choose whether people can edit or only view, then copy the link.

Microsoft's help pages also mention extras such as an expiry date, a password and a block-download option. Sources I read disagree on exactly which link types get which extra (for example, passwords are described as applying to Anyone links), and I could not confirm plan by plan entitlements. Treat these as "check what your own link settings screen offers" rather than a promise.

## Step 2: Test it the way your reader will open it

Open the link in a private or incognito window.

- If it opens straight away, anyone holding the link can see it. Great for a public handout or a club newsletter.
- If it asks for a sign-in, it is limited to people in your organisation or to specific people. Great for internal docs, but remember that outside readers will hit a wall.

Do this before you shorten, because the short link will behave exactly the same way.

## Step 3: Paste it into squish.to and name it

1. Go to [squish.to](https://squish.to) and paste your OneDrive or SharePoint link.
2. Choose your own ending, such as `squish.to/class-notes` or `squish.to/q4-budget`. Custom endings run 3 to 30 characters, using letters, numbers and hyphens, and they are case-sensitive, so lowercase is the safe choice.
3. Share it in a chat, an email, a slide or a printed handout. Every squish.to link comes with its own [QR code](/blog/qr-codes-for-every-link).

No account is needed, and each link has click stats so you can see whether it is being opened.

## Why bother, if Outlook already tidies the link?

Microsoft has said that Outlook can automatically display OneDrive and SharePoint links as a short, named file link, and you can switch back to the full URL if you prefer. That is handy inside Outlook. A squish.to link helps in the places that does not reach: a Teams or WhatsApp message, a slide, a poster, a text message, a QR code on paper. It also lets you pick a name people can read or say out loud.

## Does shortening change who can see my file?

No. A short link is a signpost pointing at your OneDrive address. It does not add or remove access. If the OneDrive link is restricted or has expired, the short link leads to Microsoft's own sign-in or access screen. Squish.to is not connected to your Microsoft account, so it never sees your files or your sharing settings. More detail in [does shortening a link protect a sensitive URL](/blog/does-shortening-a-link-protect-it).

## Small tips for smoother sharing

- **Name the ending after what it is.** `squish.to/class-notes` tells people what they are about to open.
- **Share a folder when the contents change.** The link stays the same while you add and replace files inside it.
- **Check the expiry before you print.** If the OneDrive link has an expiry date, a poster outlives it.
- **Pick view-only for handouts.** Editing is rarely what readers need.

## Quick answers

**Can I shorten a SharePoint link the same way?** Yes. SharePoint share links work the same way: create the link in SharePoint, copy it, and shorten it. Your organisation's admin settings still decide what kinds of links you can create.

**What if the short link asks people to sign in?** That comes from OneDrive, not from the short link. Change the link type in OneDrive if you want it open to everyone, then check it in a private window.

**Will the short link break if I replace the file?** If you replace the file in the same OneDrive location, the OneDrive link usually keeps working, and so does the short link. A brand new OneDrive link needs a new short link.

**Is there a cost?** squish.to is free to use, with no account required.

**Is OneDrive the only option?** No. We covered [how to shorten a Dropbox link](/blog/how-to-shorten-a-dropbox-link) too, and the steps are the same idea.

# Send an AI-Generated Sales Battlecard as a Link and See When It's Opened

To send an AI-generated sales battlecard as a link, save it as a single `.html` file or a PDF and upload it at [squish.to/upload](/upload). You get a short link that opens on any phone or laptop with no login, and a stats page that shows when the link was opened. That answers the question attachments never do: did anyone actually read it?

Any AI tool can write the card. ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, Perplexity, Grok or Mistral Le Chat will all draft a battlecard from your notes, and a page builder like v0, Lovable or Gamma can style it. This post covers the prompt, the upload, and how to read the opens honestly.

## Why a link suits a battlecard

A battlecard is only useful in the moment a competitor comes up. A PDF buried in an email thread is hard to find on a call, but a short link you can say out loud or paste into chat is easy. Because the link points at one page, you can also replace it with a better version without hunting down old attachments.

Two ways teams use it:

- **Internal:** send reps a link to the card for each competitor, then check whether it was opened before the next pipeline review.
- **External:** send a prospect a "how we compare" page and see whether it was opened before your follow-up call.

## Step 1: Ask your AI tool for a one-page card

Give the tool your real notes: pricing, strengths, known gaps, customer wins. A prompt that works across tools:

> Write a one-page sales battlecard as a single self-contained HTML file. Include: who the competitor is in one sentence, where we win, where they win (be fair), three questions to ask the prospect, and one short objection response. Keep all CSS inside the file. Under 300 words of text.

A few habits help:

- **Ask for one file.** Inline CSS keeps the page portable. Hosted HTML on squish.to can run scripts, but frames, forms, base tags and embedded objects are blocked, so skip those.
- **Fact-check every claim about the competitor.** AI tools can invent pricing or features. Verify each point against the competitor's public pages before the card goes to anyone.
- **Keep it fair.** A card that admits where the competitor is strong is more credible, and reps trust it more.

If your tool exports PDF instead, upload that. It works the same way.

## Step 2: Upload and name the link

1. Open [squish.to/upload](/upload) and choose your `.html` or `.pdf` file.
2. Pick a custom name, such as `card-acme-vs-us`. Names use letters, numbers and hyphens, from 3 to 30 characters.
3. Copy the short link. You also get a QR code, useful if the card goes on a slide or a printed leave-behind.

The limits to know: files can be up to 10MB, uploaded files stay live for 30 days, and no account is needed. HTML pages are served from a separate `file.squish.to` address, so the page runs apart from the rest of the site. Because of the 30-day expiry, set a reminder to re-upload cards you still use.

## Step 3: One upload per rep or prospect

Stats are per link, not per person. If you post one link in a team channel, you will see total opens but not who made them. For an external card, upload a separate copy per prospect, such as `card-acme-jordan`. For an internal card, one link per team or region is usually enough. The same approach is covered in [one link per recipient to track who opened a proposal](/blog/one-link-per-recipient-track-who-opened-your-proposal).

## What the stats show

Open **View stats** or visit squish.to/stats and enter your link name. You will see total opens, opens in the last 7 days, your busiest day, a 14-day chart, and recent opens with the source and the country.

Read them with some care. Chat apps and email scanners sometimes load a link automatically to build a preview, which can add an open that no human made. An open seconds after you sent the link may be one of those. Later opens, from a different source or country, are the better sign that a person read the card.

## A short message to send with it

> Quick comparison for your call with Acme: where we win, where they do, and three questions to ask. squish.to/card-acme-jordan

Lead with what it is for, and let the link carry the detail.

## FAQ

**How do I share an AI-generated battlecard as a link?**
Save it as an HTML file or PDF, upload it at squish.to/upload, and send the short link. Readers open it in their browser with no login.

**Can I see when someone opens the battlecard?**
Yes. The stats page shows total opens, the last 7 days, a 14-day chart, and recent opens with source and country.

**Can I tell which person opened it?**
Not from one link. Stats are per link, so upload one copy per recipient.

**Do link previews count as opens?**
They can. Some chat apps and email scanners load links automatically, which can add an open no person made.

**How long does the link last, and how big can the file be?**
Uploaded files stay live for 30 days and can be up to 10MB.
